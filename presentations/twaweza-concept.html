What "Twaweza" means

In Swahili, Twaweza means "we can make it happen." It is both a name and a working philosophy: change is possible when citizens, educators and governments move together.

Twaweza East Africa is a regional initiative operating in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. It combines large-scale citizen feedback, national learning assessments and evidence-based advocacy to close gaps between policy promises and lived realities.

What we do

Programmes built on evidence, voice and accountability.

Twaweza's programmes translate data into action — from village classrooms to national budgeting tables.

Uwezo Learning Assessments

Household-based assessments of children's literacy and numeracy in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Uweza tests basic competencies and publishes results to fuel public debate on learning outcomes.

Sauti za Wananchi

Strategy
Evidence in action

Data is only powerful when it reaches the people who can use it.

Twaweza collects data at scale — then turns it into accessible reports, media content, policy briefs and community conversations. The goal is not just to measure problems, but to mobilise responses.

From village-level learning assessments to nationally representative citizen panels, the methodology is rigorous, independent and openly shared.

  • Household sampling — nationally representative surveys in all three countries
  • Open data publishing — datasets and reports available for public use
  • Media partnerships — translating findings into national and local debate
How we work

Three principles that guide everything.

Twaweza's approach bridges research, public engagement and institutional reform.

📊

Measure Reality

Independent data on learning, services and citizen experience replaces assumptions with evidence.

🗣️

Amplify Voice

Citizens, especially young people and marginalised groups, are positioned as active participants in public life.

🏛️

Drive Response

Evidence and public voice are directed toward institutions with the authority and resources to act.
