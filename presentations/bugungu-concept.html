01 · A community museum

Heritage is not behind glass. It is alive.

Bugungu Heritage and Information Centre preserves and shares the cultural heritage of the Bagungu people in Buliisa District—from household tools and fishing knowledge to music, theatre and the wisdom carried in the surrounding landscape.

This concept imagines a digital doorway: a place where a young person, a school, a visitor or a research partner can first encounter that living story.

02 · Mpeehu za Bugungu

Read the wind. Read the land.

The Mpeehu za Bugungu wind map grew from a community effort to document indigenous knowledge of winds. Around Lake Mwitazinge—also known as Lake Albert—the wind is more than weather: it has helped inform fishing, navigation, farming, herding and ways of living with the natural world.

A future digital archive could introduce this story while the Centre and knowledge holders decide which details may be shared, how they should be interpreted and in whose voice.

Concept principle: community authority comes first. This visual is an interpretation of moving air—not a reproduction of the Centre’s wind map or restricted knowledge.

03 · Collection pathways

Four objects. Four stories waiting to open.

Instead of an inventory, the collection can be explored through the work, food, waters and relationships that gave each object meaning.

OBJECT PATH · 01

Mutogoolo

A traditional granary: an invitation to explore food storage, cultivation, household knowledge and continuity.

OBJECT PATH · 02

Lwoho

A wooden plate connecting material craft with the everyday rituals of preparing and sharing food.

OBJECT PATH · 03

Kiddeku

A calabash whose form opens stories about vessels, use, making and domestic life.

OBJECT PATH · 04

Kipindo kya Ngugu

A hippo spear that belongs within a larger history of the lake, subsistence and changing relations between people and wildlife.

04 · Bugungu Cultural Troupe
“An archive can also dance, sing and speak.”

The Centre’s cultural troupe brings together women, girls and men to sustain Bagungu culture through music and theatre. Performances also become a way to reach communities with education and shared concerns.

A dedicated programme page could introduce the troupe, document performances—with permission—and make it easier for schools, festivals and partners to connect.

05 · A lasting digital home

Preservation with a pulse.

The Centre’s five-year digital-preservation partnership with Africa PID Alliance points toward more than a brochure website: a durable, searchable home for community knowledge.

Built carefully, the platform could connect collection records, oral histories, learning materials and programme updates—while recognising ownership, permissions and the cultural context around every item.
