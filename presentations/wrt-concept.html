A website that makes people love rivers enough to act.

The Westcountry Rivers Trust protects and restores the rivers, streams and estuaries of the West Country. A new digital presence can turn public affection for local water into donations, volunteering, pledges and long-term support.

Why this project matters

From project-funded charity to publicly supported movement.

WRT's next chapter depends on deepening relationships with individuals, businesses and communities who care about rivers. The website needs to convert that care into action.

This concept responds to the strategic shift toward public fundraising: growing individual giving, building corporate partnerships, developing legacy giving, and making volunteering and event participation easy to join.

The design must feel as credible to a major funder or landowner as it does welcoming to a first-time donor, a wild swimmer, or a family wanting to volunteer on a Saturday.

Audience & journeys

Designed for everyone who depends on rivers.

Each visitor arrives with a different need. The site should guide them quickly to the right action without losing the emotional connection that brings them there.

Public & local communities

Discover what is happening on local rivers and find simple ways to get involved.

Individual donors

Give once, monthly, in memory or through a legacy, with clear impact and trust signals.

Volunteers

Sign up for river clean-ups, citizen science days, habitat work and local group events.

Anglers

Access fishery work, catchment reports, habitat improvements and club partnership information.

Swimmers & outdoor users

Find water quality updates, safety guidance and ways to protect the places they love.

Businesses

Explore corporate partnership, sponsorship, water stewardship and environmental reporting routes.

Funders & landowners

Review evidence, download reports and understand WRT's delivery track record across catchments.

Rivers Trust network

Connect with wider policy, research and campaigning activity across the UK rivers movement.

Brand & story

Turning science and stewardship into feeling.

WRT's identity should balance rigorous catchment science with the emotional pull of living water: the sound, the wildlife, the memories, the future.

01

Strapline & story

A clear, memorable promise that connects local rivers to everyday life. The story is not only about what WRT does, but why it matters to the people who live, work and play in the West Country.

02

Tone of voice

Warm, knowledgeable and quietly determined. Authoritative enough for funders and farmers; accessible enough for families and first-time visitors. Avoids doom in favour of agency.

03

Visual identity

River greens, water blues and earthy tones. Photography led by real West Country landscapes, wildlife, volunteers and water. Typography that feels crafted and outdoorsy, not corporate.

04

Emotive storytelling

Case studies, before-and-after catchment work, seasonal diaries and volunteer voices. Each story should end with a specific action: donate, volunteer, pledge, share, attend.

05

Trust & credibility

Charity and company numbers, membership of the Rivers Trust network, annual reports and partnership logos displayed clearly without cluttering the emotional experience.

06

Place-based belonging

The West Country is the stage. Maps, catchment pages and local event listings help visitors recognise their own river and feel part of a shared regional effort.

Website direction

Built to turn care into action.

The platform should connect content, community and conversion, integrating the tools WRT already uses or is considering.

01

Donation pathway

Clear one-off, monthly, in-memory and major-giving routes with impact framing and secure payment handling. Donor journeys should be short, trustworthy and mobile-first.

02

Event sign-up

Browseable calendar for volunteer days, guided river walks, talks and fundraising events. Simple registration with automated reminders and capacity management.

03

Volunteering

Role listings, skill-matching forms and group pages for citizen science, habitat work and local river groups. Progress tracking keeps volunteers engaged.

04

Beacon CRM forms

Embedded enquiry, donation and mailing-list forms connected to Beacon, so supporter data flows cleanly into one source of truth for fundraising and stewardship.

05

ArcGIS maps

Interactive catchment and project maps that show where work is happening, what it involves and how visitors can get involved in their own area.

06

Breathe HR jobs

Live vacancies fed from Breathe HR, with clear role pages, application instructions and working-at-WRT content to attract mission-driven candidates.

07

Reports archive

Searchable, filterable library of annual reports, catchment plans, scientific papers, policy responses and project summaries for funders, researchers and the public.

08

Content & campaigns

Flexible page builder for campaigns, appeals and seasonal moments, with social sharing, email capture and clear calls to action throughout.

Accessibility & responsibility

Built fairly, hosted lightly.

A rivers charity should practise what it preaches: inclusion, transparency and care for environmental impact.

WCAG 2.1 AA

Colour contrast, keyboard navigation, screen-reader landmarks, readable focus states and alt text for every image. Tested with real users and assistive technology.

GDPR & PECR

Clear consent for mailing lists and cookies, easy opt-out, lawful basis for processing supporter data, and privacy-by-default form design.

Low-carbon hosting

Lightweight code, compressed images, green-powered hosting and minimal third-party scripts. The site's footprint should reflect WRT's environmental values.

Timeline / approach

From discovery to handover.

A phased delivery that respects WRT's expertise, involves the team early, and leaves the charity confident running the new site.

Phase 01

Discovery

Stakeholder interviews, audience research, content audit, analytics review, competitor and peer benchmarking, and mapping of existing tools and integrations.

Phase 02

Design

Brand direction, information architecture, wireframes, visual designs for key templates, prototyping and accessibility review with WRT feedback at each stage.

Phase 03

Build

Frontend and CMS development, integration of Beacon, Breathe HR and ArcGIS, donation and event functionality, and performance optimisation.

Phase 04

Content migration

Structured migration of existing reports, pages, news and media into the new system, with redirects, metadata and search engine considerations preserved.

Phase 05

Training & handover

Editor training, documentation, launch support and a post-launch care period so the WRT team can publish, fundraise and campaign with confidence.
