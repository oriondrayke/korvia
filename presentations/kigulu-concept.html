01 · A museum of place

Not simply inside Kigulu. Made from Kigulu.

The royal family and community created a home where Basoga heritage could be encountered through the objects, skills and knowledge of the Kigulu Chiefdom.

The historic residence

A chief’s house becomes a classroom.

Kigulu Cultural Museum is housed in a historic building once used by the Chief of Kigulu. Its setting gives every visit another layer: the building itself belongs to the story being preserved.

Inside, royal regalia sits alongside musical instruments, household tools, indigenous foods and medicines, farming knowledge, craft, belief and social life.

Bulubandi · Nakigo Road · Iganga MunicipalityContact the museum before travelling to confirm visiting arrangements.
02 · Living heritage

Five trumpets. One moving melody.

Bigwala is the gourd-trumpet music and dance of the Basoga. Five or more monotone trumpets pass the melody from player to player—a musical conversation traditionally associated with royal ceremonies and now also performed on social occasions.

Its place on UNESCO’s List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding makes transmission especially important. A future website can help introduce the form, connect learners to approved recordings and centre the voices of practitioners.

UNESCO urgent safeguarding list · 2012
The animated forms are a graphic interpretation of interlocking voices, not a reconstruction of museum instruments or a substitute for a live performance.

04 · Museum as classroom

Move learning beyond the textbook.

Community museums allow students to examine objects, compare oral accounts, practise games, discuss changing customs and meet people who hold specialised knowledge.

A school-facing website could turn a visit into a complete learning journey—from preparation materials for teachers to follow-up activities and student research prompts.

05 · Digital heritage

Give each story a lasting address.

Kigulu Cultural Museum’s connection to Africa PID Alliance’s cultural-heritage work points toward a deeper digital future: records that can remain discoverable, attributed and linked without separating objects from their cultural context.

A practical first version could join collection records, school resources, approved oral histories, research requests and museum updates in one low-maintenance platform.

Digital access is not automatic public access. The museum and community determine what may be recorded, who may see it and how knowledge holders are credited.
